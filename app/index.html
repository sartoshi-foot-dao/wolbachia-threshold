    Two strategies, one lever

    Both sexes: infected mothers pass the bacterium on, so the street converts and stays converted. Virus-blocking mosquitoes replace wild ones, and female numbers fall only about 10%.

    Males only: nothing inherits it. The failed eggs thin the wild population only while releases continue, and it rebounds when they stop. In the Singapore trial the article cites, male releases cut mosquitoes by 85% and dengue by roughly 70%.

    Why it saturates

    Wolbachia passes only through eggs. An infected male can't transmit it, but his matings with wild females produce eggs that don't hatch. That penalty falls only on wild females, because infected females have viable, infected young with either kind of male.

    The advantage grows with the infected share, while the egg cost stays fixed. At low share the cost wins and the bacterium fades. Past the tipping point the mating advantage wins, and the share climbs on its own after releases stop.

    What this model assumes

    About 2,600 adults on a 1 km by 640 m street grid. Each day 10% die and the rest wander about 22 m. Females mate once, with a random male within 40 m, and lay every 4 days in the nearest container, where larvae compete for 10 days. Three wild adults a day arrive from the map edge. There are no seasons, insecticides or disease, and released males compete equally.

    Check against theory: 20 runs were started at each of several shares around the calculated tipping point, for egg costs of 10%, 25% and 40%, and followed until the infection took over or died out. With no wild arrivals, half the runs take over 2 to 4 points below the formula, because crowding inside each breeding container helps the infection more than patchy local mating hurts it. Wild arrivals push it back up by 1 to 3.5 points. The post has the tables, and the check script reruns them.

    Mechanism and Singapore results from Maya Rosen, Mosquitoes are a choice, Works in Progress. The article gives no threshold. The default cost is set to match the roughly 25% reported for the wMel strain in Turelli and Barton, 2022. Other values are illustrative.
